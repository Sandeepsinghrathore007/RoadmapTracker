## Why This Order (backward design logic)

Full composite mock tests don't start Month 1 because without foundational pattern recognition in Reasoning and a solid geographical framework of Rajasthan, speed drills only lead to guesswork and negative marking. Complex Data Interpretation (DI), multi-variable puzzles, and State E-Governance schemes don't start Month 1 either, because DI relies on mental percentage/ratio fluency, and E-Governance policies tie directly into state administrative history and geography mastered in Weeks 1-12. 

So:
- **Weeks 1-4 (Phase 1):** Core Patterns, Coding, Machine Logic (Inequality & Input-Output) & Rajasthan Geography.
- **Weeks 5-8 (Phase 2):** Relational/Deductive Logic, Dictionary Word Ordering & Rajasthan Art and Culture.
- **Weeks 9-12 (Phase 3):** Clocks, Calendars, Dice, Non-Verbal Figure Series & Rajasthan History and 7-Stage Integration.
- **Weeks 13-16 (Phase 4):** Data Interpretation (DI), Data Sufficiency, Rajasthan Polity, State E-Governance & Full Official PYQ Speed Drills.

That's the entire logic. Everything below fills in the days.

## How To Use This Document (read once, then never again)
1. **Active daily dual-track routine (2 to 2.5 hrs/day).** Allocate 60-70 minutes to the daily Reasoning task (20-25 active problems) and 50-60 minutes to the daily Rajasthan GK task (theory + 15 PYQs).

## Common Mistakes (read before you make them)
1. **Drawing complete tick marks for Ranking:** Drawing 50 lines on rough paper instead of using direct formula Total = (L + R) - 1.
2. **Calculating full numbers in Data Sufficiency:** Wasting 2 minutes finding the exact numerical value when the question only asks whether statements (1) and (2) together are sufficient.
3. **Misinterpreting Blood Relation genders:** Assuming a person's gender from their name (e.g., assuming 'Kiran' or 'Sonu' is female/male without a specific relational word).
4. **Getting trapped in Verbal Assumptions:** Choosing an assumption because it is "morally good" or "a general fact", even if it is completely outside the premise of the given statement.
5. **Miscalculating Pie Chart Angles:** Forgetting that 360° = 100%, leading to multiplying percentages directly by degrees without dividing by 3.6.
6. **Confusing Jan Aadhaar ID Digits:** Confusing the 10-Digit Family ID with the 11-Digit Individual Member ID.
7. **Neglecting the 5th Option 'E' in RSMSSB:** Leaving options un-darkened on OMR practice sheets, inviting a 1/3rd negative marking penalty.
8. **Blindly reading thick GK books without PYQ filtering:** Spending 5 hours memorizing obscure king genealogy trees instead of high-yield topics (UNESCO Forts, Folk Deities, Bijolia, 7-Stage Integration, E-Governance).

**Conceptual Traps**
- *Trap:* Thinking a century year is always a leap year. *Correction:* Century year must be divisible by 400 (e.g., 1900 is NOT a leap year; 2000 IS a leap year).
- *Trap:* In clocks, assuming the hour hand stays fixed at the hour mark as minutes pass. *Correction:* The hour hand moves at 0.5° per minute (30° per hour).
- *Trap:* Confusing 'Facing Inward' vs 'Facing Outward' in circular seating. *Correction:* Facing Inward (Left = Clockwise), Facing Outward (Left = Anti-Clockwise).
- *Trap:* Confusing Koppen's BWhw (Arid Desert - Jaisalmer/Bikaner) with BShw (Semi-Arid Steppe - Nagaur/Jodhpur/Sikar).
- *Trap:* Assuming the capital of Matsya Sangh was Bharatpur. *Correction:* Matsya Sangh capital was Alwar; Dholpur ruler (Udaybhan Singh) was Rajpramukh.
